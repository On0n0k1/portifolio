### Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment (Assessment A):** Craft specificity is high; concept specificity is medium. DESIGN.md's discipline is real and verified in the rendered page — every gold value is pixel-sampled, the kit-frame border-image and CSS clip-path are never mixed on one element, fonts are only declared at imported weights, touch targets hit 44px, contrast measures ~10-11:1. But the "adventurer's record" concept itself doesn't derive from what makes Lucas's practice distinctive (cryptography, ZK-proofs, distributed systems). This exact system could dress an unrelated profession's CV with equal internal coherence — well-executed and non-generic in craft, but taste-driven rather than domain-derived in concept.

**Deterministic scan (Assessment B):** The static CLI scan (`detect.mjs --json src public`) came back clean — 0 findings. That's expected; it only sees source files, not computed/rendered state. The **browser-overlay scan** (DOM + computed styles, injected into the live page) found **26 raw signals**, which resolve to three real categories:
- **19× `line-length`** — every `<li>` in `.job__highlights` (Experience/Projects bullets) runs ~131 chars/line at 1400px, well past the ~50-75ch convention. Confirmed by direct visual crop: the bullet column is 992px wide with text running edge-to-edge.
- **2× `text-overflow`** — `h3.talent-branch__title` overflows its box on "Orchestration & CI/CD" (+25px) and "Cryptography" (+18px) — no `overflow:hidden` on the parent, so it bleeds into padding rather than clipping, but it's a real, measured overflow (`scrollWidth` > `clientWidth`). **This independently corroborates Assessment A's separate, source-level critique of the Skills section from a completely different angle** — one flagged it as a content/weighting problem, the other caught it as a pixel-level overflow, on the same section.
- **5× `dark-glow`** (really 4 distinct + 1 duplicate signal from a page-level re-scan of the same CSS declaration) — flags the hero readout digits' text-shadow glow. **Verified false positive in context**: this is the site's single documented, deliberate authored-motion moment (`sigil-glow`), explicitly named in DESIGN.md's Elevation & Depth section as the one exception to an otherwise flat-by-default system, built from a token (`color-mix(... var(--color-amber) ...)`, not an arbitrary value), and already verified for contrast earlier this session. The generic rule correctly flags text-shadow glow as a category smell; here it's the intentional, restrained exception the system itself documents. No fix needed.

Where the two assessments converge independently is the strongest signal in this report: **both flagged the mobile nav's hidden overflow** (A from heuristic/persona analysis, B from direct visual inspection of the rendered page) and **both flagged the Skills section** as the page's weak point (A on content/weight grounds, B on pixel overflow). Two unrelated methods landing on the same two problems is a stronger signal than either alone.

**Visual overlays:** Assessment B's browser injection succeeded and captured real findings, but it ran in its own throwaway headless Chrome session (no native browser tool was available), and it stopped its live server afterward per the workflow's cleanup requirement — there's no overlay currently visible in your own browser. ### Overall Impression

The site's floor is high — real contrast math, real touch targets, a licensed asset used with unusual discipline, zero static anti-patterns. The gap is concentrated almost entirely in one section: Skills. It's structurally the least-designed part of an otherwise well-composed page, and it happens to sit right where the site's actual differentiator (crypto/blockchain) lives, buried 6th of 10 cards deep. Fix that one section and the two nav/discoverability issues, and this moves from "good with one rough edge" to genuinely tight.

### Priority Issues

**[P1] Skills section buries the stated differentiator under unweighted structure — confirmed from two independent angles.**
- **Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md names crypto+blockchain+distributed-systems as "the differentiator versus a generic backend engineer," but Blockchain/Cryptography sit 6th/7th of 10 equal-weight cards, with the 14-item AWS card and 9-item Backend card ahead of them. A skimming recruiter (this surface's entire audience, per PRODUCT.md) is likely to stop before reaching what actually sets Lucas apart. Independently, the browser scan measured two of these same cards' titles ("Orchestration & CI/CD," "Cryptography") physically overflowing their layout box, and the grid's `align-items: start` with wildly uneven item counts (3 vs. 14) leaves two empty column-slots in the final row.
- **Fix:** Reorder groups to lead with Blockchain/Cryptography/Distributed Systems; consider a compact "focus skills" row above the full card wall reusing the existing gold/stat treatment; fix the title overflow (give `.talent-branch__title` room or allow wrapping); rebalance the grid (dense packing or capped group lengths).
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`, then `/impeccable clarify` for the reordering/emphasis question.

**[P1] `STAT_PATTERN` regex visually splits "S3" into two colors mid-word.**
- **Why it matters:** `src/App.tsx`'s stat-highlighting regex has no left-boundary check against preceding letters, so it matches the trailing digit in "S3" and renders it as plain "S" + gold "3" — confirmed at two locations in the Deckers Brands bullet list. A recruiter evaluating AWS expertise will recognize "S3" instantly; seeing it visually broken reads as a bug in the exact domain being demonstrated.
- **Fix:** Add a non-letter lookbehind boundary, e.g. `(?<![A-Za-z])\d[\d,]*...`, or denylist alphanumeric product tokens.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

**[P2] Mobile nav's hidden sections still lack a discoverability cue — confirmed independently by both assessments.**
- **Why it matters:** This was partially addressed in tonight's earlier `/impeccable adapt` pass (the nav went from silently clipping 4 of 6 sections to being genuinely scrollable, with a CSS scroll-shadow affordance). Both assessments, working independently and by different methods, still flagged it: the shadow is apparently too subtle to read as "swipe for more" at a glance. PRODUCT.md explicitly names mobile-from-LinkedIn as a real access path.
- **Fix:** Add a visible trailing chevron/arrow icon, or a more pronounced affordance than the current gradient shadow alone.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

**[P2] Experience/Projects bullet lines run ~131 chars — well past the readable measure.**
- **Why it matters:** 19 confirmed instances across `.job__highlights`, on the site's core proof-of-work content, at roughly double the ~50-75ch convention for comfortable reading.
- **Fix:** Constrain the bullet list's max-width independent of the panel width, or introduce a two-column layout at wide viewports.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

**[P3] Musashi Miyamoto precept block has no framing connecting it to the rest of the page.**
- **Why it matters:** Positioned directly before the Contact CTA with zero context, it reads as scope creep to an unfamiliar recruiter rather than a meaningful personal touch — adds scroll length with no clear signal payoff at exactly the moment before the conversion action.
- **Fix:** Add one framing sentence, relocate it, or cut it.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`
